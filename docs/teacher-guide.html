Teacher guide · v01.0005

Small steps. Clear progress.

Choose a skill and level, collect a short check under consistent conditions, then keep the score and response details together. The site supports classroom goal checks, home practice, and your shared Excel workflow.

Start a check

  1. Open the skill from the homepage. Use its level or task choices to match the student's current target.
  2. Choose Baseline, Weekly Check, or Practice. Enter a student code, goal label, goal revision, and support notes if useful. These can also be completed on the report.
  3. Keep the displayed Form ID for the report, or choose a new form. Press Start check when the student and administrator are ready.
  4. Use the page's response controls. The one-minute chime is a cue, and the student may continue beyond it. Stop the check when the intended observation is complete.
  5. Review the report. Record the level and conditions alongside the score; then use Copy Excel Row, Download Score CSV, or the print option.

Forms and timing: a Form ID repeats the random sequence within the same activity and level. It helps you reproduce a check; it does not mean that two forms have been validated as equally difficult. A one-minute rate and an accuracy score describe different things. Compare like tasks and like conditions.

Administration details that matter

  • Oral sound skills: an adult presents the spoken task and marks the response. Keep the adult prompt area out of the student's view when it would reveal the answer.
  • Spelling: the adult sees the target and presents it aloud. Do not let the student read the target word while spelling it.
  • Reading: note whether the adult read the passage or question aloud. Listening comprehension and independent reading are different conditions.
  • Supported writing: choosing a word or arranging supplied tiles is supported sentence work. Keep its score separate from independent composition.
  • Independent writing: the result stays Pending until the teacher explicitly rates all four rubric criteria. A Pending result is not a zero. Keep the writing sample with its score.
  • Sentence order: reviewed alternatives can be accepted. The teacher can approve another grammatical arrangement without creating a second attempt. Formal checks use one check per sentence; Practice can include retries.
  • Fonts, pictures, prompts, and help: record the support used. Keep it consistent across observations intended to be compared.

One-minute fluency reads

Choose the passage, student, and read/day before starting. Mark errors with the existing right-click or keyboard tools and select the last word read. The report keeps the actual total time and the whole-read rate.

To retain a separate first-minute score when continuing longer, mark the word reached at the one-minute chime and use the page's Save minute mark control. That records the selected endpoint for the minute; a later whole-read endpoint can then be different. If a minute endpoint was not saved, treat the first-minute field as unavailable rather than guessing it from the final rate.

Connect the reports to your shared workbook

The updated workbook keeps the five original historical sheets and adds Score Log and Progress Summary. Each new assessment is one row in the log. New months and new students do not require a new website template.

  1. Open the connected workbook in the shared location your team normally uses.
  2. On a test report, enter the Student code, Subtest / goal label, and Administrator. Use a consistent Goal ID and Goal revision for observations that belong together.
  3. Choose Copy Excel Row. In Score Log, select column A of the first blank data row and paste once.
  4. The export fills A–Z, the 26 imported fields. Leave the calculated helper columns AA–AD in place; do not paste over them.
  5. Use the Progress Summary filters for student, goal, revision, score type, date window, level, and mode. Practice is kept out of formal summaries.
Three staff members, one log: use the same shared workbook and coordinate the blank rows you paste into. The website prepares a row or CSV; it does not directly edit or synchronize a OneDrive, SharePoint, or local Excel file. This avoids handing each staff member a competing updated copy of the master workbook.

When students, goals, or months change

  • New student: enter the new student code on the report and paste the new row. No website roster edit is needed.
  • Updated IEP goal: use a new Goal revision, such as the IEP date. Keep older revisions in the log so their scores do not merge with a different goal.
  • New month: change the summary date window. New records already contain dates; the new log does not depend on monthly tabs being created first.
  • Different level or support: record the change and use the level filter when comparing like observations.
  • Historical monthly sheets: their existing entries remain in place. New pasted rows feed the new summary, not the old weekly cells automatically.

Combine CSV results from the team

When copying directly to the shared workbook is inconvenient, each administrator can choose Download Score CSV. Open Score Export Tools, select the collected CSV files, review the combined results, then copy the rows or download one combined CSV.

The helper uses Record IDs to remove exact duplicates. A conflicting row with the same ID is flagged for review rather than silently replacing another result.

If you regrade a result that is already in Excel, update its existing Record ID row instead of appending it again. The workbook excludes duplicate IDs from its summary until the duplicate rows are resolved.

Dates and scores: Copy Excel Row sends a native Excel date value, and the log displays it as a date. CSV files use readable YYYY-MM-DD dates; when importing CSV into Excel, set Date to a date type and Score to a number. Accuracy and rubric values are fractions—0.8 means 80%—while WCPM is an ordinary number. A real zero score stays zero; an ungraded or unattempted score stays blank.

Keep student details in your workflow

The public site contains no student roster from your workbook. Student codes, notes, and results are entered during a session and remain in that page until copied or downloaded. The score tool does not upload them or remember them after the page closes. Keep your score files and connected workbook in the location your school uses for student records.
